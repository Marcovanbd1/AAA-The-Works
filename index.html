The design discipline

Emotional Architecture is a design discipline that treats the emotional climate of a human encounter as primary data. In other words: the science of what actually happened in the room.

We apply it before, during and after everything we build. It is the interpretive foundation underneath Neighbourhood Signals, Support Playlists, and every piece of infrastructure CoLab produces.

This is where ambient voice technology is heading. We got there through practice.

Before signals can emerge, conditions must exist

You can't build a listening system into a community that hasn't been given anywhere to speak.

Four listening apertures must exist first. Each one catches a different kind of signal — because people speak differently depending on where they feel they are.

The Peer Layer

People find their voice when they see themselves reflected. A space where community speaks to community — not upward to a system, but sideways to each other. That lateral speech is some of the most honest signal a neighbourhood produces.

The Share Layer

A resource is only a resource if people can find it. When navigation is frictionless and gatekeeping is absent, people move freely — and the patterns of that movement tell you something the referral data never will.

The Private Layer

Some things are only said when no one official is listening. The low-stakes, unobserved moment — a screen at midnight, a quiet corner — produces a quality of disclosure that formal channels structurally cannot reach. That's not a gap in the data. That's the most important part of it.

The Hub Layer

Not a showcase. Not a directory. A place that works — so people return, and in returning, leave traces. Built for utility rather than completeness, it becomes the gravity at the centre of the system.

We built a working version of each of these in Weymouth and Portland. You can build your own. They don't have to look like ours.

Build the four conditions. Then listen to what emerges.

Neighbourhood Signals

A neighbourhood listening to itself.

When the conditions exist, signal emerges. Not extracted, not surveilled — heard.

Neighbourhood Signals is the interpretive layer that reads what the conditions have made visible. Everyday digital behaviour — searches, navigation paths, dead ends, loops, pages abandoned at midnight — turned into neighbourhood-level intelligence that Integrated Neighbourhood Teams can actually act on.

No names. No histories. No personal data. Everything aggregated, anonymised, pattern-level, place-based.

Tested through a 91-day pilot in a live neighbourhood. Three signal clusters identified and handed to the INT Partnership Group before they appeared in any clinical metric.

This is what ethical AI in public services looks like. Not a black box making decisions about people. A quiet infrastructure that helps a community notice itself — early enough to respond proportionately rather than reactively.

Support Playlists

The What Next problem. Solved.

You shout it, whisper it, rant it, slur it or sob it. One shot. You say what's going on. You get a Playlist.

A calm, bespoke, sequenced set of next steps. Built from what you just said. Shaped by Emotional Architecture — designed for your actual state, not your assumed optimal one.

Not in the clinical record. Not integrated with anything. Platform agnostic by design.

A GP at the end of a consultation. A discharge nurse with a patient who has a folder they can't face. A community worker with someone who doesn't know where to start. A QR code on a hospital exit. On every hospital bed. On your fridge. On a pub toilet door.

The appointment isn't for three weeks. The QR code is still there.

Wherever someone stops and thinks I don't know what to do next — that's where a Playlist lives.
